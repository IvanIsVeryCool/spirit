# Nueva Spirit Cabinet website: project handoff

This is the context for any coding session on this repo: what the site is, how it's built, what's already decided, and what's next.
It is written for Claude Code, which reads `CLAUDE.md` from the repo root automatically.
The README covers the owner-facing basics: how to add a car and how to update scores.

## What it is
- **Owner:** Ivan Cui (GitHub `IvanIsVeryCool`), a student on Nueva School's spirit cabinet.
- **Hosting:** **Cloudflare Workers** (static assets), at https://spirit.ivancui211.workers.dev (Worker `spirit`, connected to GitHub `IvanIsVeryCool/spirit`, branch `main`). Push to `main` and Cloudflare deploys it in about a minute; there is no build step.
  `wrangler.jsonc` serves the repo root as static files; `.assetsignore` keeps the repo-only files (`CLAUDE.md`, `README.md`, config, `worker/`) off the site, and `_redirects` sends those paths to `/` as a backstop.
  A small Worker (`worker/index.js`, `main` in `wrangler.jsonc`) sits in front: requests for files are served straight from the assets (the Worker doesn't run), and everything else goes to it; it handles `/api/` (the gallery's photo submissions, below) and hands any other path back to the assets (`env.ASSETS`). Submissions are stored in Workers KV, the `PHOTOS` binding, which has no `id` in the config: Cloudflare's automatic provisioning (Wrangler 4.45+) created the namespace on the first deploy and keeps it linked; Git deploys don't write the id back. The review password is the `REVIEW_PASSWORD` secret, set by the owner in the dashboard (Workers & Pages → spirit → Settings → Variables and Secrets).
  It moved from Netlify on 2026-10-02, when the Netlify team ran out of credits and production deploys paused; the old address (`https://moonlit-crumble-d56590.netlify.app`, `netlify.toml`) is frozen on an older version.
  The sandbox's network policy blocks `*.workers.dev` and Netlify, so live checks are done by the owner.
- **`/` (hub):** a 3D golden-hour train platform.
  - **First visit of the day:** a paper ticket loader ("Board with sound" or "Board without sound").
    Then a first-person cutscene: you sit on a bench holding the ticket and look around, and a red-and-silver double-deck commuter train pulls in.
  - **Train cars:** each car's door is a section of the cabinet. A split-flap departures board (a station-hall timetable: time, destination, car, status) lists the same cars.
  - **The cars, front to back:**
    - **Photo Gallery** (car 1) opens inside the car, over its interior, on the same page (the owner didn't want it to go to another page; see "Photo Gallery" below). `/gallery/` is the same gallery as a stand-alone page, used only by the static version.
    - **Weekly Newsletter** (car 2) and **Events** (car 3) are in-scene visits, like the cab (see "Weekly Newsletter and Events" below). In Weekly Newsletter you sit down in the lower deck and read the newsletter as a newspaper; in Events you look up at the car's next-stops screen, where a little train runs a line whose stations are the events.
    - Spirit Points used to be car 1; the owner swapped it for the gallery. The standings are now reached through the officer ("Leaderboard"): clicking him (or his label) has him point straight to a billboard past the end of the train, where the camera pans and the full board opens, on the same page (see "The leaderboard billboard").
  - **Meet the Cabinet** (the last car) is an in-scene visit, not a page: you board, walk down the lower deck to the back of the car, the cab door slides open and the cabinet are waiting in the cab with their names above them (see "Meet the Cabinet" below).
- **`/points/`:** the live Spirit Points leaderboard, in the same golden-hour world.
  - Nothing on the platform links here any more (the officer's billboard shows the same board); it's for direct visits and the static version. Opening it (first time per session) plays a short ride (about 7 s to arrival): the train runs north on an illustrated 3D Peninsula map, Nueva → Hillsdale, then at a junction the line fans out into one branch per car (each with its own terminal, named from `DOORS`) and the train takes the first branch, whose terminal `app.js` names "Spirit Points" (`Ride({ title })`). A chime at each stop, a split-flap "Next stop" display and a route strip.
  - At Spirit Points it pulls in beside a big station billboard; the standings and results open on its face.
  - Scores come from a Google Sheet. (The old igloo-style scroll page was replaced, at the owner's request.)
- **Photo submissions:** "Submit a photo" in the gallery (both the platform's and `/gallery/`) opens a form (`gallery.js`: the photo, a caption, the event, an optional name, and a box to tick that it's OK to post). The browser shrinks the photo to 2000 px and re-saves it as a JPEG first (quicker, and it drops the photo's hidden details, like where it was taken), then posts it to `/api/photos`. Nothing is public until the cabinet approves it on **`/review/`** (password: `REVIEW_PASSWORD`); approved photos come back from `GET /api/photos` and the gallery adds them after the ones in `photos.js` (album "From the school" if none was given; "Photo: name" in the lightbox). The Worker checks the file really is a JPEG, PNG or WebP (its first bytes), allows 4 MB, six submissions an hour from one address, and pauses submissions while 60 are waiting; ten wrong passwords in an hour lock the review page for that address.
- **`/gallery/`:** the stand-alone gallery page (the platform opens the same gallery in car 1, without leaving the page): the sky gradient, the sign back to the platform, album filter chips (only with more than one album), a masonry of photos (CSS columns), and a lightbox (grows out of the photo you tap, FLIP; arrows, swipe, ←/→/Esc, focus kept inside). `gallery/photos.js` lists the photos (`file` in `assets/gallery/`, `caption`, `album`, `date`). It opens from the warm light of the train door (`#cover`). The first photos are renders of the Spirit Line itself, until the owner adds real ones.

## Running and testing locally
- Serve the repo root with any static server, for example `python3 -m http.server 8770`, then open http://localhost:8770.
  ES modules need http, so opening the files directly from disk won't work.
  A plain server has no `/api/`: the gallery shows only `photos.js`, and the photo form says submissions aren't open. To run the Worker too, use Wrangler from the repo root: `npx wrangler@4 dev --var REVIEW_PASSWORD:test` (local KV, kept in `.wrangler/`, which is git-ignored). Careful with `pkill -f` patterns in the same shell command as the thing you start: they match their own command line.
- three.js r160 is vendored at `/vendor/three.module.min.js`, with add-ons in `/vendor/jsm/`
  (postprocessing: bloom and output passes; RoomEnvironment).
  Pages use an importmap that maps `"three"`. Don't add a bundler or npm dependencies.
- `#debug` on the hub URL exposes the scene as `window.__st`.
- **Headless testing** (Playwright Chromium with `--use-gl=angle --use-angle=swiftshader --enable-unsafe-swiftshader`):
  - The software renderer is slow: about 2–5 fps, and loading takes 1–2 minutes because of the warm-up.
  - Once the 3D is running on the hub, new CSS transitions never start in this headless setup (they stay pending at `currentTime` 0; the timeline itself runs). Real browsers are fine. To check the layout of something that fades in, add a style tag with `transition:none!important` for it in the test.
  - To land screenshots at exact moments of the cutscene, step the clock in the page:
    `__st.clock.getDelta = function(){ this.elapsedTime += .1; return .1 }`.
    Cutscene time is `__st.clock.elapsedTime - __st.intro.t0`.
- **Daily ticket reset:** the date of the last visit is stored in `localStorage['spirit-hub-day']`.
  Delete that key to see the ticket and cutscene again, or use the "Replay intro" button.
- Fonts: Archivo (display), Public Sans (text) and DotGothic16 (LED and dot-matrix text), all from Google Fonts.

## How the hub works (things that are easy to break)
- **Warm-up (`Station.warm`)** runs behind the loading screen.
  - It builds the cutscene rig, compiles every shader, and renders every object once (frustum culling off, train in view, camera on the bench).
  - Without this the train stutters as it first enters the view. Anything new added to the scene is warmed automatically, as long as it exists before `warm()` runs.
- **Car interiors (`interior.js`):** built inside `_side()`, so they exist before merging and `warm()`.
  - Trim (the partitions' red accent line, the yellow step and threshold edges) stands clearly off its surface (≥ 6 mm, ends not flush): flush or 2–4 mm off, it flickered as you walked through (z-fighting). The same for the toy doors' white stripe.
  - No two boxes may share a face: where one part meets another, end it at the other's face (or inside it), never on the same plane. The black-and-red flicker beside the doors was the upper-deck slab beside the stair well ending exactly on the left partition's face, its dark end fighting the partition and the red line; the stair well's wall, the headers over the openings and the right upper deck's floor (a zero-height box on the ceiling's top) did the same. To check, collect each car's triangles and look for overlapping ones on the same axis-aligned plane facing the same way (only hidden ones should be left: undersides, insides of other parts).
  - Each car gets its own copy of the `base`, `seat` and `glow` materials; `render()` scales their colour with the door's opening and hover (`d.inside`): the lights are on from the start (you see in through the windows), a little brighter as the doors open, warmer on hover.
  - The platform-side window glass (`M.window`, also the door-leaf windows) is a tinted transparent physical material with clearcoat reflections, over real openings in the body.
  - The car-end gangway blocks reach 15 cm into each car, so the interior ends at `END = CAR_L / 2 - .22`.
  - The bogies sit under the lower deck. The springs and dampers were lowered (invisible from outside) so the lower floor (`LOWER = .43`) clears them; keep bogie parts below that.
  - Window and far-door glass is a shared transparent material with a warm gradient, so the evening outside shows through softened.
- **Crowd (`crowd.js`):** built in the constructor before `warm()`.
  - Walkers spawn off-screen at x = ±44 and cross; a few start mid-platform so it's busy on arrival. Slots, speeds and outfits are random; the same-lane walker behind slows down rather than overlap.
  - Lanes are placed so passers-by stay below the doors on screen from the resting camera: desktop walkers at `front + 16.4 / 17.2`, the bike path at `front + 20.2`; phones (camera closer) use walker lanes at `front + 11.3 / 12` and no forecourt bikes. The road cyclists ride at z −8.85 and −12.4.
  - When boarding, walkers near where the camera's flight line crosses their lane hurry out or wait (`flX` in `update`).
  - Each person is one draw call (plus its shadow); the whole scene is ~330 calls on desktop with the shadow pass, ~105 on phones.
  - Bench sitters are characters slim enough behind (`sitters()`), seated with `seatAt()` so their backs (or packs) just meet the backrest instead of passing through it. Each rolling suitcase gets its colour once, at spawn.
  - `Person.update()` resets the head to its rest pose before the mixer runs, because some clips (like `sit`) don't animate the head and the look turn would otherwise build up every frame.
- **Merging:** after the scene is built, `mergeStatic()` collapses each car, the props, people and wires.
  - Anything animated, or referenced later, must have `userData.keep = true` so it isn't merged.
    Examples: door leaves, door hit boxes, light spill, nose mesh, headlight beam, people's heads.
  - This keeps the scene around 300 draw calls.
- **Light level:** ACES tone mapping at exposure .84, hemisphere .72, sun 1.85, bloom .4 (the owner asked for less light overall). The planters hold Nature Kit bushes (`city.js` swaps the stand-in blobs that `scenery.js` marks `userData.bush`).
- **Per-frame HTML over the 3D** (the officer's label, the floating sound button, the crew's name tags, the billboard's board): placed with `transform` only. Their widths are measured once (`widthOf()` in `hub.js`, cleared on resize and when the sound button's words change), so the loop never forces a layout.
- **Door signs:** each car's LED sign has its two pages (destination and "NOW BOARDING", then "CAR 0n") drawn once into two textures (`led.pages`); `render()` swaps `led.mat.map` as it flips. `drawLED()` is a few thousand canvas dots, so it only runs at build and in `redrawSigns()` (once the fonts load). `warm()` uploads both pages.
- **Resizing mid-visit** (a window resized, a phone turned): `Station.resize()` calls `trip.resize()`, which recomputes the visit's field of view (the cab, the events screen) and the newspaper's reading distance; on the billboard, `hub.js` re-runs `layoutBoard()` and `station.reframeBoard()`.
- **Background tabs:** both pages suspend the audio while hidden and resume it on return (if sound is on).
- **Loading:** `index.html` `modulepreload`s the station's modules (and the vendored loaders it needs), so they download in parallel while the ticket shows, instead of each waiting for the module that imports it. Without JavaScript, a `<noscript>` hides the loader and links to Spirit Points and the gallery.
- **Watch for:** a trailing `// comment` on a line that also holds code swallows anything after it on that line. ## How Spirit Points works
- **Boot:** a warm cover (the same gradient as the hub's boarding flash) while fonts, data and the scene load; `ride.warm()` renders from the start and from the billboard.
- **Ride or not:** it rides the first time per browser session (and if you come through a train door, `sessionStorage['spirit-door']`, though no car links here now); a reload in the same session goes straight to the board (`sessionStorage['spirit-rode']`). Skip button or Escape skips. Reduced motion or no WebGL: `body.static`, the board is the page.
- **Rendering:** once the camera has settled in front of the billboard (`state === 'board'`), the 3D stops redrawing (it's mostly under the board and nothing in view moves); a few frames are drawn as it settles and after a resize (`redraw` in `app.js`).
- **Billboard fit:** `layoutBoard()` picks the board's box for the screen (wide on desktop, tall on phones), `setBillboard()` reshapes the 3D billboard to that aspect, `finalPose()` places the camera so its face fills that box, and `placeBoard()` lays the HTML panel exactly over the projected face every frame.
- **Sound:** the hub's `StationAudio` (`audio.ride()` is the running hum, `chime()` at stops, `doors()` on arrival, `clatter()` for flaps). On unless turned off (`localStorage['spirit-sound']`), unlocked on the first tap or key.
- **Data:** `data.js` fetches the sheet; scores are cached in `localStorage['spirit-cache-<sheetId>']`; it re-checks every `refreshSeconds` and when the tab returns; changes flip only the changed flaps and show a toast.
- **Testing without the sheet:** the sandbox can't reach Google, so seed the cache key above with sample entries (`{ entries: [{ row, challenge, t, points: { sr, jr, so, fr } }] }`).

## Design rules (the owner asked for it to look less "vibe coded")
- One family of controls: corners `--r` (6 px; 4 px for small plates and chips), one shadow `--lift`, Archivo 700 at about 12.5 px, uppercase with .1em tracking. No pill shapes, no glows, no bouncing or pulsing attention-getters, no rainbow effects.
- Labels in the world are station plates: the officer's "Leaderboard" is a navy plate with a small bar-chart icon and a pointer; the floating sound button is a white plate with a pointer; the crew's name tags are white with a red top edge.
- Back buttons carry a left chevron (`.back`). Buttons are white (secondary) or Spirit red (primary).
- The ticket's stub is printed, not shiny: a red validation seal (the logo in a ring), "Single journey", the barcode and serial.
- Focus rings show for keyboard users only: `softFocus()` in `hub.js` moves focus to the next control only after keyboard use (`keyNav`); `:focus-visible` is a 2 px amber outline.
